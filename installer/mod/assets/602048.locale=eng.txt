The Bronze Star Medal recognizes heroic or meritorious achievement or service in connection with military operations against an armed enemy. Under the Navy rules used during the Korean War, direct participation in combat operations could authorize the bronze Combat Distinguishing Device, the letter V. Only one V was worn on a ribbon, however many awards involved combat.

Further awards of the same decoration were shown by gold stars. When the V and stars were worn together, the first star was placed to the wearer's right of the V and the second to the left. In the career rules, the V represents a demanding combat sortie below the Silver Star threshold; the star variants preserve the pilot's total number of Bronze Star awards whether merit or valour came first.
