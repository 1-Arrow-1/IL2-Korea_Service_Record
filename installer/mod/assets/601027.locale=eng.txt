The Senior Pilot badge is the second of the three grades of the United States Air Force pilot badge. It is distinguished from the basic pilot's badge by a star centred above the shield.

The graded system was introduced by the Army Air Corps in the early 1940s to mark experience and length of rated service, and was carried over when the Air Force became an independent service in September 1947. The rating is conferred on pilots who have completed roughly seven years of rated service and accumulated some 2,000 flying hours.

By the Korean War many squadron and flight leaders wore senior wings, most having gained their first operational experience in the Second World War.
