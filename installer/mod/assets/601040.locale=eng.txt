The Command Pilot badge is the highest of the three grades of the United States Air Force pilot badge. A star enclosed within a laurel wreath is worn above the shield, distinguishing it from the plain badge and from the star-only senior grade.

Introduced with the graded rating system by the Army Air Corps in the early 1940s, the award recognises long service in the air rather than a single act. It is conferred on pilots with roughly fifteen years of rated service and some 3,000 flying hours.

In Korea the badge was largely the mark of group and wing commanders, career officers whose flying hours had been accumulated over a decade or more, in many cases beginning in the Second World War.
