IL-2 Korea Service Record 1.8.0 — the squadron's war diary, and a seating chart that
fits your regiment


Gentlemen,

Two things in this one. A new page that tells the story of your squadron day by day,
and a fix to the Career Helper that anyone flying a Soviet, Chinese or North Korean
unit needs.


THE WAR DIARY

There is a new button beside the flight record on a pilot's page. It opens the
squadron's war diary: the whole career, written up a day at a time.

    17 April 1951
      Mission 14   Attack Frontline   Hagalgye · 15 targets destroyed   ★
        — Hugh Gillespie lost his aircraft
        — Jose Castro lost his aircraft
        — Jose Castro was killed in action           [KIA]
        — Hugh Gillespie was killed in action        [KIA]
        — You earned and were presented with the Distinguished Flying Cross
        3 decorations earned, squadron-wide · 2 presented

Every line is something the game wrote down. Nothing is generated, nothing is invented.
Sorties and where they went, who reported for duty, who was wounded, who was lost,
which aircraft arrived and which came out of the shop, when an operation began and
ended, and when the squadron was ordered to move to a new field.

The missions you flew yourself are marked, and your own decorations are the only lines
in colour, so you can find your own thread down a long career at a glance. Quiet days
collapse to a single line — one aeroplane back on strength does not deserve the same
space as the day you lost four.

It prints, and the diary stops at the day your career has reached. It never shows you
tomorrow, even though the game sometimes writes tomorrow down in advance.

Three things I had to get right, which are worth knowing because they would each have
made it lie:

  * An award event is not an award. Of the 1,718 in the career I built this against,
    244 are decorations earned, 229 presentations, and 1,245 are removals — the
    bookkeeping behind a cluster replacing the medal it supersedes. Counted together
    they said the squadron was decorated 99 times on a day it was decorated 29.
  * The game writes some casualty rows twice. One pilot's Mustang is logged as lost
    twice in the same second on the same sortie. The diary folds those; it does not
    fold kills, because two kills in one second really are two things destroyed.
  * An unscheduled sortie is numbered -1, -2 and downwards internally. It says
    "Unscheduled" rather than "Mission -1".

One discovery along the way. Event type 30 was unidentified until Alex checked what the
game was telling him: it is the squadron being ordered to move to another airfield, and
the diary now says so by name.


THE DISPLAY CASE, TO KEEP

The shadowbox has two buttons under it now: Save picture and Print.

The picture comes out at 2050 x 1860 — the size the artwork was drawn at, not the size
of your window — with the engraved plate and everything in place, named after the pilot
and his squadron. Post it wherever you like.


THE CAREER HELPER: EASTERN REGIMENTS

This is the important one if you fly for the VVS, the PLAAF or the KPAF.

An American squadron is 24 seats: six flights of four. An eastern regiment is not the
same thing with fewer men in it — it is a commander and his wingman, then three
squadrons of twelve, thirty-eight positions in all. The seating chart only ever drew
24 of them. If you flew a MiG regiment or a ShAP, a third of your unit was invisible.

It now reads the establishment from your own career file and draws the whole thing:

    Commander Flight
    1st Squadron                        2nd Squadron
    3rd Squadron

Six columns, and the tab scrolls, so the bench and the buttons stay reachable. Every
part is named the way the game names it, in your own language.

CORRECTION (28 September): an earlier version of this post said the game could not
show you the 3rd Squadron at all. That was wrong - the Combat Units screen does
scroll sideways to reach it. What is true is that the game keeps at most 24 pilots
active and benches the rest, so an eastern regiment normally sits well under its own
establishment and those far squadrons are usually empty. My apologies for the error.

My thanks to Swoose, who reported that his 57th ShAP had 38 positions and asked whether
the chart could hold them. It could not, and chasing that turned up three further
defects in the same code.


THE CAREER HELPER: REVIVING A PILOT

Two changes here as well.

Your own pilot can now be revived. The Captured tab has always been willing to mark
your own commander missing — that is what it is for — but Revive only ever listed your
squadron mates, so marking your own man was a one-way door. One of you walked through
it. He is on the list now.

And the window widened from the day of the loss to that day and the day after. The
mistake this tab exists to undo is nearly always noticed one click too late, with End
Day already pressed. One day and no more, though: a man brought back a week on walks
into a squadron whose war went without him. Past the window, the backup the Helper
writes before every change is the way back.


THE MOD

Unchanged from 1.7.0, byte for byte. Nothing to merge or redo.
