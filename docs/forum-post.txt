[size=5][b]IL-2 Korea Service Record — a pilot's file for your career, plus an awards fix[/b][/size]

Gentlemen,

I have been flying careers in IL-2 Sturmovik: Korea and wanted the sort of record the
paperwork would have kept: what my pilot shot down, who he shot down, what he was decorated
for, and what the flak did to him on the way home. The game holds all of it and shows very
little of it, so I built something that reads it out.

It comes with a second thing I did not plan on. While chasing a medal that never arrived I
found real bugs in the game's award definitions — six decorations that cannot be granted at
all, one of them broken in two separate ways. The installer offers the fix as an optional
component, and I would encourage you to take it. More on that below.

[b]This is read-only.[/b] It never writes to a career file and cannot cost you a campaign.
Careers only for now — scripted campaigns and single missions are not read.


[size=4][b]What it shows[/b][/size]

[b]The pilot[/b]
[list]
[*]Rank insignia, squadron badge, date of birth, status and health
[*]Skill, discipline and courage, with their boosters — the characteristics the game tracks
but only half shows
[*]A portrait you can add yourself, cropped to a passport frame
[/list]

[b]Combat record[/b]
[list]
[*]Aircraft, vehicles, railroad, armaments, buildings and shipping, broken down by target type
[*][b]Confirmed victories[/b] — every air-to-air kill of the career, numbered, with the
opponent's aircraft, [i]his name[/i], the date and the altitude
[*]Air kills by type, split between those shot down and those caught on the ground
[*]Combat efficiency: best sortie, victories per sortie and per flight hour, average
engagement altitude, mission and career score
[/list]

[b]Mission debriefings[/b]
[list]
[*]Take-off and landing times from the flight log, and how the sortie ended — landed, landed
damaged, force-landed with the aircraft written off, bailed out, or failed to return
[*]A combat log per sortie: what was destroyed, when, by whom, at what height
[*][b]Battle damage in the same timeline[/b] — when you were hit, how many strikes in that
pass, how much of the aircraft was gone by the end of it, and what did it where the game
recorded that. Roughly nine times in ten the game logs no attacker, so I show nothing rather
than invent one.
[*]Every pilot on the mission, each with their own damage timeline
[/list]

[b]The squadron[/b]
[list]
[*]The full roster — portraits, rank, highest combat decoration, status, victories, sorties,
hours and characteristics — sortable on every column
[*]Click a pilot for his own record; click a medal for the full-size art and the game's own
description
[/list]


[size=4][b]Install[/b][/size]

[list=1]
[*]Run the setup program. It asks for your language first — that becomes the application's
language, and you can change it later from any page.
[*]Choose [b]both[/b] components: the Service Record and the Awards Mod.
[*]Point it at your IL-2 Korea folder. Setup usually finds it. It is the folder containing
[i]data\Career[/i].
[*][b]Enable modifications in the game[/b] — Settings, General, Enable modifications.
Without this the game never looks at modded files and the awards mod does nothing at all.
Setup warns you if it sees the setting switched off.
[*]Fly a mission, then open the Service Record. The first launch takes a few seconds while it
reads the game's art and text; after that it is instant.
[/list]

Uninstalling removes both cleanly. The mod is additions only — nothing in your game is
overwritten or replaced, so deleting the files simply returns the game to reading its own
archives. Your careers and the medals already in them are untouched either way.

Note that verifying game files in Steam removes loose mod files. If your medals stop
appearing after a game update, run setup again.


[size=4][b]Why the awards fix matters[/b][/size]

Short version: [b]in the stock game you can never be decorated twice for the same kind of
action[/b], and the first decoration comes down to a dice roll rather than the sortie that
earned it.

The game evaluates awards down two separate paths, [i]AwardInProc[/i] and [i]AwardByDef[/i].
Six USAF decorations are dead on both:

[list]
[*]Silver Star, 2nd award
[*]Silver Star, 3rd award
[*]Distinguished Service Cross, 2nd through 5th award
[/list]

Their [i]AwardInProc[/i] carries a condition, [i]noAwards=1[/i], that never becomes true in a
career, and their [i]AwardByDef[/i] is [i]RND<0[/i], which never fires by design. Neither route
can ever grant them.

[b]One of them is broken a second time, independently.[/b] The Silver Star's 2nd award
requires that you already hold the Silver Star — but the file names the wrong one:

[code]
601019   RequiredAward = "603018"      should be 601018
601020   AwardRemove   = "(ExAw=603019)"   should be 601019
[/code]

601 is the Air Force ladder; [b]603 is the Marine Corps[/b]. So the Air Force's second Silver
Star waits on the Marines' Silver Star Medal — and those ids have names in the locale files
but [b]no award definitions at all[/b], so the prerequisite points at nothing. Either fault
alone is fatal. I confirmed it the slow way: a controlled test with three clean airborne kills
and no competing award, and nothing was granted.

The base Silver Star and the Distinguished Service Cross [i]do[/i] arrive in the stock game,
but only through [i]AwardByDef[/i] — a 10% roll against your career total, not against the
sortie you actually flew. In my own career the Silver Star landed on a 10% roll the day my
career total first reached three victories, while the sortie that earned it was ignored. The
Medal of Honor is not guarded and works as intended; it simply asks a great deal — six
airborne victories in one sortie, or five while wounded.

[b]What the mod changes[/b]
[list]
[*]Removes the [i]noAwards[/i] condition from the affected USAF valour awards, so the sortie
you flew is what decorates you
[*]Corrects the two 603 references to 601
[*]Bands the valour tiers by score, so one outstanding sortie awards one appropriate medal
rather than three at once
[*]Makes two decorations reachable that exist in the stock file but had no route at all
[/list]

The communist ladders have [i]noAwards[/i] in places too, but always inside an OR, so they are
harmless and I have left them alone.

[b]Three decorations added[/b]
[list]
[*]Senior Pilot Wings and Command Pilot Wings — the qualification badges the stock game leaves out
[*]Medal of Honor with Oak Leaf Cluster, for the second award
[*]A missing promotion step, so rank progression no longer skips a rung
[/list]

New artwork is drawn into the game's own award atlas, and every decoration carries a name and
description in all six languages.


[size=4][b]Languages[/b][/size]

English, Deutsch, Español, Français, Русский and 中文 — the same six the game ships. Medals,
ranks, mission types and target names come from the game's own files, so they read exactly as
they do in the career screens next door.

Each career can also be set to its own language, independently of the application. A German
player flying a USAF career can read that man's record in English — his medals, his rank, his
mission types — while everything else stays German.


[size=4][b]Requirements and notes[/b][/size]

[list]
[*]Windows, and IL-2 Sturmovik: Korea installed. No Python or other runtime needed.
[*]The Service Record never writes to your game. Pilot photographs and its own settings live
under %LOCALAPPDATA%\IL2KoreaTracker.
[*]The application ships [b]no game content[/b]. It reads the art and text it needs from your
own installation each time.
[*]The awards mod is an ordinary set of loose files. If you would rather install it by hand,
the full list and destinations are in [i]Awards mod - manual install.txt[/i].
[/list]


Feedback, bug reports and requests are all welcome — particularly from anyone flying a
communist career, since this has had far more hours against a USAF one. And if you manage a
Medal of Honor, I would like to hear about it. I have not managed it yet.

Clear skies.
