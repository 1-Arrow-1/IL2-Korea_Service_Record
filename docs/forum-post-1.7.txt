IL-2 Korea Service Record 1.7.0 — a seating board for the squadron, and what taking the
career engine apart says about who should sit where


Gentlemen,

1.7 adds one screen and answers a question I have wanted answered since I started this:
what does the game actually do with a pilot's numbers? The new screen is a seating board
for the squadron. The answer turned out to be more interesting than the screen, so most of
this post is the answer, and you can use it whether or not you ever open the tool.

The short version, for anyone who reads no further:

  * Skills 5 and skills 4 fly identically. The cap is 4.
  * Only one man's "boosters" count on a mission — whoever holds the lowest-numbered
    seat you send. Everybody else's are dead weight for that sortie.
  * Courage buys kills and costs lives, in the same table, in opposite directions.
  * Fatigue does nothing at all. Nothing in the game reads it.
  * And seating barely matters. A perfect arrangement is about four per cent better
    than a sensible one, so do not lose sleep over it.

The mod is unchanged from 1.6.1 — same awards.cfg, byte for byte. If you are on 1.6.1 and
do not use the Career Helper, there is nothing here you need.


THE FLIGHTS BOARD

The Career Helper has a new tab. It draws your squadron the way the Combat Units screen
does — six flights of four, the two sections of each flight ruled apart, the alert flight
outlined — and puts on every card the numbers you would otherwise have to open twenty
pilot files to read: name, tail number, his own skills / discipline / courage in the order
his panel prints them, what the game will rate him as in the air, his boosters, and
whether he is fit to fly at all.

Underneath sits the reserve, best first, with the same numbers. That is the part I wanted
for myself: you can see at a glance whether somebody on the bench out-flies somebody in
the line-up. On my squadron two men did.

You rearrange it by clicking. Click a pilot, click another, they swap. Click a man on the
bench and then a seat, and he takes the seat and its aeroplane while the man he replaces
goes down to the pool. There is a button that proposes a whole seating, and a button that
throws your changes away and reads the career back from disk. Apply writes it, after
taking a backup, in one transaction, and refuses outright if IL-2 has the file open.

It will not let you do a few things, all for the same reason: an exchange must be one for
one. You cannot add a twenty-first man to a twenty-aircraft squadron, because the
twenty-first would have nothing to fly. You cannot bring up a man who is in hospital or
still walking home. Those are not fussiness — they are what keeps an airframe from being
stranded in a slot the game never looks at.


HOW A SQUADRON IS ACTUALLY ARRANGED

There is no "flight" or "section" anywhere in a career file. There is one number per
pilot, his slot, and everything else is arithmetic on it:

    flight  = slot / 4          six of them: Red, Blue, Green, Yellow, White, Black
    section = slot / 2          twelve of them, two per flight

So slots 0-3 are Red, 4-7 Blue, and so on, and within each flight the first two seats are
one section and the last two the other. Reserve pilots live at 2000 and up, the dead at
5000 and up, and 1000 and up means a man who is off the strength for some reason.

**The aircraft belongs to the man, not the seat.** I tested this by swapping two pilots in
game and diffing the file: both the pilot slot and the aeroplane slot moved, so the man
kept his own tail number rather than inheriting the seat's. Any tool that reseats pilots
has to move the pair.

**The alert flight is not fixed.** It defaults to flight 6 but you can put the D anywhere,
and it is stored as a plain list of seats — `watchmen = slot|20|21|22|23`. Duty belongs to
the seats, not the men, so moving people around changes who stands alert without changing
the assignment.

**The reserve is a queue.** A man sent down is appended to the end; when one is promoted,
everybody above him shifts down to close the gap.

One find I did not expect: your career file keeps a log of every rearrangement you have
ever made. Every time you commit a change on that screen the game writes a row recording
the complete pilot-to-slot and aeroplane-to-slot mapping, plus the alert list. Mine had
forty-eight of them, and diffing consecutive rows reconstructs every promotion, demotion
and loss of the campaign so far. That is where the rules above came from — the game's own
record of what it did, rather than me guessing.


WHAT THE GAME READS WHEN IT DECIDES

Two different things happen with a pilot's numbers, and they use different ones.

**In the air, only skill counts.** When the game builds a mission it writes an AI level
for each of your wingmen. I fitted it against every pilot in my own generated mission:

    AI level = his displayed skill, capped at 4, one lower if he is hurt

Which means the thing worth saying plainly: **a pilot at skills 5 flies no better than one
at skills 4.** The fifth point is wasted in the air. Courage and discipline do not appear
in the mission file at all — they are for the campaign's own reckoning, below. And a
wounded man measurably flies worse, which is the one bit of the model that rests on a
single observation, so treat it as likely rather than certain.

**Off screen, all three count, and so does the commander.** There is a file in the
campaign data, ranks-effectiveness.cfg, holding three tables:

                     attribute 1 ....... 5
    FightKills   skill       0.10 → 0.30
                 courage     0.00 → 0.20
                 discipline  0.00 → 0.20
    BombKills    skill       0.05 → 0.25
                 courage     0.00 → 0.20
                 discipline  0.00 → 0.20
    Losses       skill       0.20 → 0.00      more skill, fewer losses
                 courage     0.00 → 0.20      more courage, MORE losses
                 discipline  0.20 → 0.00      more discipline, fewer losses

I could not find anything that read that file, and for a while I assumed it was dead. It
is not. The function that uses it gives itself away in its own error message —
"Ranks-effectiveness.cfg: no %s koefs block" — and it is asked for three categories by
name: fightkills, bombkills, losses. What it does is this:

    if there is a commander, and he is not this pilot:
        skill      = min(4, his skill      + the commander's skill booster)
        courage    = min(4, his courage    + the commander's courage booster)
        discipline = min(4, his discipline + the commander's discipline booster)
    otherwise use his own three unchanged

then it looks each one up in that category's table and adds the three numbers together.

Read the Losses row again. **Courage is the only attribute that cuts both ways**: it buys
kills and it costs lives, by the same amount, in the same table. A bold squadron scores
more and buries more. That is a deliberate and rather good piece of design, and nothing in
the game tells you about it.


THE COMMANDER, AND THE PURPLE ARROWS

Those little purple up-arrows on a pilot's card — the game calls them boosters — are what
he gives the men flying with him. They are not part of his own ability; he never boosts
himself.

**Exactly one man's boosters apply to a mission: whoever holds the lowest-numbered seat in
the force you send.** Not each flight's leader, not the section leaders. One man, covering
every aircraft that goes.

You can watch this happen without any tools. Send different combinations from the mission
screen and the three purple chips at the top change to match whoever ends up in command —
Blue's leader if Red stays home, Green's if Red and Blue both do. I checked four
combinations, predicted the fifth before looking, and it came up right. Then the code said
the same thing: the function above is handed the first pilot in the group list as the
commander for every man in it.

Two details worth knowing.

The chips are printed in the panel's order — **skills, discipline, courage** — which is
not the order the values are stored in. If you are comparing what the screen says against
anything else, the middle two are crossed over.

And the boosters come from **promotions**. An ordinary decoration raises one of a pilot's
own three attributes; a promotion raises one of his boosters instead, capped at 4, picking
the attribute the same way. So the men who have been promoted most are the men worth
putting in charge — which is at least a pleasing bit of design.


SO WHERE SHOULD PEOPLE SIT?

Here is where I have to be honest, because I set out to build a clever optimiser and the
arithmetic talked me out of it.

Everything above is real, but the numbers are small. One booster point is worth 0.05. The
full range of an attribute is 0.20. So raw ability outweighs a booster four to one, and
the booster is clamped: **a pilot already at 4 gains nothing from it.** Six of my twenty
are at skill 4 already and two are at 4/4/4, for whom no commander's boosters do anything
whatsoever.

I scored my squadron's best eight-aircraft force — eight being the cap on very nearly
every mission type; the twelve-ship ones are relocations — and then hill-climbed to find
the best arrangement that exists:

    as it is now        5.05
    the best possible   5.25      +4%, and losses rise by the same proportion

Four per cent, cancelled out by the extra losses. Meanwhile the arrangement that looks
most obviously right — strongest man in slot 0 and down from there — scored **ten per cent
worse** than what I already had, for a reason that is only obvious once you know about the
cap: it stacks your best pilots under yourself, and if their courage and discipline are
already at the ceiling then your boosters are thrown away on them.

So the tool proposes one seating and offers you no choice of philosophy, because I
measured the choice and it is not worth making. The rules it uses:

  * you keep your own seat;
  * lead seats go to the best boosters, then the best fliers, and never to anyone the
    game would rate below 3 in the air;
  * wingman seats go on ability;
  * nobody unfit is brought up from the reserve, and the alert seats get nobody hurt;
  * and when two men are equally good for a seat, the one already sitting in it stays,
    because a suggestion you have to undo by hand is worse than no suggestion.

The one thing worth doing by hand, which no algorithm fixes: if you are choosing where to
put your best-boosted leader, put him with men who still have room to grow. Not with the
5/5/5s. They cannot use it.


FATIGUE, WHICH TURNS OUT TO BE NOTHING

I built a rest threshold into the board and then took it out again, and I think the reason
is worth writing down.

Fatigue is real and it moves: it runs 0 to 9, a sortie adds one, a day on the ground takes
one back. I found both of those in the code and they are not in doubt.

What I could not find was anything that reads it. Every reference to the field is a write.
No other part of the game queries the pilot table at all. Nothing in the generated mission
varies with it — two of my pilots flew at fatigue 6 and were rated exactly the same as men
at 2. And the game does not display it.

I want to be careful here, because I made exactly this claim about the boosters and was
wrong: I had said no code read those either, and then found the function that does. The
difference is that for the boosters I had missed something — the reading function sat
right next to the database routines and I passed over it without opening it. For fatigue I
have since gone back and enumerated every single instruction that touches the field, and
they are all writes.

So: it is recorded, it is never read, and the tool ignores it. If somebody can show me an
effect, I will happily put it back.


PILOT STATES, WHICH I ALSO GOT WRONG

Worth a line because it bit me. A pilot's state is not just alive or dead. 0 means
available; 1 means walking home after going down, unhurt, with the time he gets back
recorded; 2 killed; 3 missing; 4 in hospital. A man at state 1 can still be sitting in a
line-up seat — the game leaves him there and greys him out — so a seat number on its own
never tells you a pilot can fly. My first version cheerfully promoted my best pilot into
the line-up while he was still walking back across Korea.


THE REST OF 1.7

**Present every pending award at once.** The Award points tab now lists every decoration
your pilots have earned and not been given, with their standing beside them, and hands
them all over in one go — costing the award points it should, adding the attribute point
the game adds, writing the same event the game writes. The killed and the missing are
listed in red and not ticked: a posthumous award is a decision, not something to slip past
by accident.

**The Career Helper looks like the Service Record now** rather than a grey utility
bolted to the side of it, and it closes when you close the Service Record, from the page
or from the tray. That last one was embarrassing to have missed.

**Aircraft and materiel on the squadron panel** are now laid out properly, aircraft on one
side and stores on the other, with drawn icons rather than text glyphs.

**A parsing fault of mine, fixed.** Six stock awards — the Order of the Red Banner in all
three awardings, Hero of the Soviet Union, the Order of Lenin and the DPRK Hero's Medal —
write their condition across two lines. The engine joins them back together; I was reading
only the first half, which made the top Soviet and Korean decorations look malformed when
they were nothing of the sort. My error, not the game's.
