IL-2 Korea Service Record 1.7.0 — a seating board, and what the career engine actually
does with your pilots' numbers


Gentlemen,

1.7 adds a seating board to the Career Helper. More usefully, it comes with answers to
something none of us can see from inside the game: which of a pilot's numbers the engine
reads, and what it does with them.

Five findings, and none of them are guesses:

  * **Skills 5 flies exactly like skills 4.** The value is capped at 4 before it reaches
    the mission. The fifth point buys nothing in the air.
  * **Only one man's boosters count on a sortie** — whoever holds the lowest-numbered seat
    in the force you send. Every other leader's are dead weight for that mission.
  * **Courage buys kills and costs lives**, by equal amounts, in the same table.
  * **Fatigue does nothing.** Nothing in the game reads it.
  * **Seating is worth about four per cent**, so do not agonise over it — but the
    arrangement that looks most obviously right is ten per cent *worse*, for a reason
    below.

The mod is unchanged from 1.6.1, byte for byte. If you do not use the Career Helper there
is nothing here you need.


WHAT THE ENGINE READS

Two separate reckonings, using different numbers.

**In the air: skill alone.** When a mission is built, each of your wingmen is given an AI
level. It is his displayed skill, capped at 4, one lower if he is hurt. Courage and
discipline never reach the mission file. That cap is why a 5 flies like a 4.

**Off screen: all three, plus the commander's boosters.** The campaign resolves AI results
through a table of coefficients:

                     attribute 1 ....... 5
    FightKills   skill       0.10 → 0.30
                 courage     0.00 → 0.20
                 discipline  0.00 → 0.20
    BombKills    skill       0.05 → 0.25
                 courage     0.00 → 0.20
                 discipline  0.00 → 0.20
    Losses       skill       0.20 → 0.00     more skill, fewer losses
                 courage     0.00 → 0.20     more courage, MORE losses
                 discipline  0.20 → 0.00     more discipline, fewer losses

Read the Losses row twice. Courage is the only attribute that cuts both ways: the same
0.20 that buys you victories buys you funerals. A bold squadron scores more and dies more,
and nothing in the game mentions it.

Before that lookup, the commander's boosters are added to each man's three attributes and
the result is clamped at 4.


THE COMMANDER

The purple arrows are what a man gives the pilots flying with him. He never boosts
himself, and **exactly one man's apply to any mission: the occupant of the lowest-numbered
seat in the force.** Not each flight's leader. One man, covering everything you send.

You can confirm this without the tool — the three chips on the mission screen change to
match whoever ends up in command as you vary which flights go. I checked four
combinations, predicted a fifth before looking, and then found the code doing the same
thing.

Two things the game does not tell you. The chips are printed **skills, discipline,
courage**, which is not the order the values are stored in, so the middle two are crossed
over against anything else you compare them to. And the boosters come from **promotions** —
an ordinary decoration raises one of a man's own attributes, a promotion raises one of his
boosters instead. The most-promoted men are the ones worth putting in charge.


SO WHERE SHOULD PEOPLE SIT?

I set out to build a clever optimiser and the arithmetic talked me out of it.

A booster point is worth 0.05. The full span of an attribute is 0.20. Ability outweighs
boosters four to one, and the booster is clamped, so **a pilot already at 4 gains nothing
from one**. Six of my twenty are at skill 4 and two are at 4/4/4, for whom no commander
does anything at all.

Scoring my best eight-aircraft force and then searching for the best arrangement that
exists:

    as it stood         5.05
    best possible       5.25      +4%, and losses rise by the same proportion

Four per cent, cancelled by the extra losses. Whereas stacking your best men from slot 0
downwards — the obvious arrangement — came out **ten per cent worse**, and the reason is
the cap: it puts your strongest pilots under yourself, and if their courage and discipline
are already at the ceiling your boosters are thrown away on them.

So the board proposes one seating and offers no choice of doctrine, because I measured the
choice and it is not worth having. The one thing worth doing by hand: put your
best-boosted leader with men who still have room to grow, not with the 5/5/5s.


FATIGUE, WHICH IS NOTHING

It moves — 0 to 9, a sortie adds one, a day on the ground takes one back — and nothing
reads it. Every reference to the field is a write. Nothing in the generated mission varies
with it: two of my pilots flew at 6 and were rated the same as men at 2.

I am wary here, because I said the same about the boosters and was wrong — I had missed
the function that reads them, sitting next to the database routines. So for fatigue I went
back and enumerated every instruction that touches the field. They are all writes. If
somebody can demonstrate an effect I will gladly put the rest threshold back.


THE BOARD ITSELF

Your squadron laid out as the Combat Units screen lays it out, with the numbers you would
otherwise open twenty pilot files to read on every card — attributes, what the game will
rate him as in the air, his boosters, whether he is fit. The reserve sits underneath, best
first, so you can see whether somebody on the bench out-flies somebody in the line-up. On
mine, two did.

Click two pilots to swap them; click a man on the bench then a seat to exchange them. A
button proposes a whole seating. Apply takes a backup and writes in one transaction, and
refuses while IL-2 has the file open. Exchanges are one for one, so nobody is ever left
without an aeroplane.


THE REST OF 1.7

**Present every pending award at once**, from the Award points tab — the full list with
each man's standing beside it, handed over in one go, spending the points and adding the
attribute point the game adds. The killed and the missing are listed in red and not
ticked; a posthumous award should be a decision.

**The Career Helper is dressed like the Service Record** now instead of being a grey
utility beside it, and it closes when you close the Service Record.

**Aircraft and materiel on the squadron panel** are laid out properly, with drawn icons.

**A parsing fault of mine, fixed.** Six stock awards — the Order of the Red Banner in all
three awardings, Hero of the Soviet Union, the Order of Lenin and the DPRK Hero's Medal —
write their condition across two lines, which the engine joins back together and I did
not. That made the top Soviet and Korean decorations look malformed when they were fine.
