IL-2 Korea Service Record 1.4 — ribbon racks, eighteen more decorations, a Bronze Star
with the V, and a fix for a citation that could never be earned


Gentlemen,

1.4 is out. Everything below is new or corrected since 1.3; what the tracker and the mod
already did is in the earlier posts and has not changed.


THE RIBBON RACK

Every pilot now wears his ribbons. Under the portrait on the service record, and in the
roster's pilot card, there is the rack as it would sit on his tunic: rows of three, highest
precedence top-left, the short top row centred, with the devices on the bars — bronze and
silver oak leaf clusters, the V, the bronze and silver service stars of the Korean Service
Medal — at the sizes the regulations give them, and a unit citation with its gold frame.
Unit citations are worn on the right breast, so they form their own small rack beneath the
decorations.

The order of wear is the Army's of 1950–53, which the Air Force followed until it had a
manual of its own. One thing that surprises people: the Purple Heart sat *below* the
Commendation Ribbon then; it moved up only in 1985.

Soviet and North Korean pilots have racks too, on the 24 × 8 mm bar those armies used
(the American bar is 1⅜ × ⅜ in., a different shape, and the rack shows it). A second or
third Order of the Red Banner is a second or third order, worn as another identical bar —
there were no clusters in that system. The North Korean Orders of Freedom and Independence
and of Soldier's Honour have a different bar for each class, as the originals do. Chinese
pilots get no rack, on purpose: the People's Volunteers' awards were pin badges, and the PLA
wore no ribbon bars until 1955. I would rather show nothing than invent something.


THE AWARDS

Eighteen more decorations, all things a USAF pilot in Korea could actually wear:

 • The Bronze Star with the "V". Army regulations of 1950 put a bronze V on the ribbon of a
   Bronze Star awarded for heroism — worn once, however many valorous awards followed; every
   further Bronze Star, for valour or merit, added a cluster. The mod now tracks both: how
   many, and whether one was for valour. The V is earned by the sortie that was hard rather
   than the one with the biggest score — one enemy aircraft in a fight that went badly, a
   strike of twenty-five or more targets pressed through the flak, or a mission finished
   wounded — each with a chance, never a certainty. Three tiles: V, V and one cluster, V and
   two.
 • Silver Star, fourth and fifth awards (three and four clusters). Real, if rare — Robin Olds
   had four — and needed, because an aggressive pilot fills the first three in weeks.
 • Distinguished Unit Citation, third and fourth (two and three clusters), and the ROK
   Presidential Unit Citation with one, two and three clusters at 200, 300 and 400 squadron
   missions.
 • Distinguished Service Medal — the Army pattern, since the Air Force had none until 1960 —
   for a commander who has reached general rank and held the squadron for half a year. The
   one decoration the flag ranks from the promotion mod can earn.
 • National Defense Service Medal. Created 22 April 1953 for everyone in uniform since June
   1950: nothing happens for two years, then the whole roster gets it on one morning.
 • Commendation Ribbon with Metal Pendant, and three clusters. The Air Force Commendation
   Medal did not exist until 1958; in Korea airmen received the Army's, green with five white
   stripes. It is the award below the Bronze Star for the man who does the unglamorous work
   well — the wingman who has flown his missions, held his position and brought his aircraft
   home. Fifteen missions and a one-in-five chance each sortie after that; clusters at 45,
   75 and 110.

Every one has its name in six languages, a description in the medal screen (three written
fresh, the clusters pointing at their base decoration as the game's own do), and artwork in
the mod's own atlas rather than squeezed into the game's.


CORRECTED

 • The Distinguished Unit Citation could never be earned. 1.3 defined it on one mission's
   kills, which is how the DUC was historically given — but the game's squadron-award path
   evaluates an empty sortie record, so every per-sortie value is zero there. I found this by
   watching a 15-vehicle squadron sortie earn nothing, then reading the game's own code to be
   sure. The DUC now marks the point where a squadron at full efficiency has destroyed 50
   aircraft or 600 vehicles, vessels and buildings — a milestone rather than a day, which is
   the only shape the engine allows. My own squadron earned it on 23 April 1951, so it works.
 • The Silver Star and Distinguished Service Cross are now single-sortie awards with a
   chance rather than a certainty: two kills, 60 %; three, 80 %; four or five for the DSC.
   A pilot can no longer collect a Silver Star and a DSC for the same fight.
 • Why a `noAwards` guard never works, if you have looked at the awards file: the plain Air
   Medal is re-granted and retired again every sortie once a pilot holds a cluster, and that
   silently clears the flag for everything below it in the file. The mod avoids the token;
   the note is in the file for anyone who mods it further.


ALSO

 • Earlier rungs of a ladder are no longer lost. Behind each ribbon in the awards list there
   is a small arrow; it unfolds the decorations that ribbon replaced — the plain Silver Star
   under its cluster, the five Air Medals under the silver cluster — each with its own
   dates. The squadron's citations show every rung on one row.
 • Rank descriptions. The game has none. Click any rank insignia and there is now a
   paragraph on it — what the insignia was, where the rank stood, and what a pilot of that
   rank was doing in Korea — for all 32 ranks of the four nations, in six languages. The
   Chinese ones say honestly that the PLA had no ranks until 1955 and the game applies them
   backwards.
 • "Close the Service Record" in the header. Closing the browser tab leaves the tracker
   running in the tray, which even I found non-obvious; this stops it properly.
 • The "highest combat award" on the roster now ranks the new decorations correctly (the V
   ladder above the merit Bronze Star, the Commendation below the Air Medal).

Existing careers pick the new rules up at the next day rollover; nothing needs restarting.
The awards file changed shape considerably this time, so if you edit it yourself, diff before
you merge.
